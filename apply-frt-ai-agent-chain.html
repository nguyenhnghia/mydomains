AI Agents problem at FRT that I deeply care

The problem

Today, AI tools at FRT are still mostly one-off chatbots or isolated copilots. The bigger opportunity I keep coming back to is this: every single person at FRT — from back-office staff to store sales associates — should have their own "agent assistant team" quietly working alongside them. Not a single mega-bot, but a small crew of role-aware agents that knows that person's job, their tools, and their daily flow.

Without that, productivity gains stay siloed in a few power-users. With it, AI stops being a feature and becomes a layer of the company.

My approach

Build a reliable, templated agent team — one standard architecture that can be quickly deployed and widely adapted across departments, instead of a custom build for every team.

  • Reusable core: a coordinator agent (like Marvin in my current build) plus a library of specialist agents (research, drafting, ticket-ops, reporting, etc.).
  • Role kits: each department (sales, finance, HR, supply chain…) gets a pre-tuned bundle of skills, prompts, and integrations — not a blank canvas.
  • Skills as the unit of behavior: every action an agent takes lives in an inspectable skill, so business owners can read, edit, and approve what their agents are allowed to do.
  • Safe-by-default ops: human-in-the-loop checkpoints, audit logs, and rollback — so we can deploy widely without losing control.

The core idea: treat agents like hires

The mental model I most believe in: agents are not tools, they are new hires. They should go through a real, end-to-end onboarding — the same way any of us would when we join a new team — so they understand how we actually do the work before they're asked to help.

Concretely, an agent's onboarding would include:

  • Read the playbook: ingest the team's SOPs, glossaries, internal docs, and recent decisions — not just public knowledge.
  • Shadow real work: observe live tickets, emails, meeting notes, and customer interactions (read-only) to learn what "good" looks like in context.
  • Pair with a buddy: a human mentor reviews and corrects the agent's draft actions during a probation period — same as onboarding a junior teammate.
  • Graduate by evaluation: the agent only unlocks autonomous actions after passing a measurable rubric (accuracy, safety, tone), per skill.
  • Continuous review: regular "performance reviews" — metrics, user feedback, drift checks — with the option to retrain or retire skills, just like managing a real teammate.

If we get this right, every employee at FRT walks into work with a small, trusted team that already knows their job — and the whole company compounds from there.

AI stuff I built recently

I built an AI agent team — a small crew of cooperating agents running locally on my machine.

Stack: multica orchestrates the crew, with kiro-cli and claude-cli as the underlying agents.

Core idea: a custom agent scans my real Jira tickets, reads each ticket's state, and takes the right action automatically. The decisions are driven by skills I author — so the behavior is fully controllable and inspectable, not a black box.

Current state: only one agent — Marvin, the coordinator — is reliably working today.

Ambition: grow this into a full agent team that runs independently under my supervision — each agent owning a slice of the workflow, with me as the human-in-the-loop.
